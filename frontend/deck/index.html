Sealed bids, cleared by an enclave that forgets them.

A desk with a block to move has to show its size to find a price. Buta runs that auction inside an attested enclave, so the party running it cannot read a bid, cannot quietly drop one, and keeps nothing once it has cleared.

01 / the problem

Finding a price for size means showing the size

A desk wants to move a block of FXRP. To find a price it shows the size to five counterparties. One of them trades ahead of it. So price discovery for size stays in chat rooms: un-auditable, slow, and resting on trusting whoever holds the spread.

Who this is for: OTC desks, treasuries and market makers moving size on Flare.

02 / why the obvious fixes fail

A public contract cannot read a sealed bid

A contract that cannot read the bids cannot compute a second price. To know every bid on chain is to publish every bid.

Commit and reveal does not rescue it. A losing bidder who dislikes the outcome simply never reveals, and the clearing stalls. Everyone who does reveal publishes their size.

03 / what it is

A sealed-bid desk where the auctioneer is blind

Buta is a sealed-bid OTC desk on Flare Confidential Compute. Bids are encrypted to an attested enclave, which opens them, clears at the Vickrey second price, and returns only the winner and that price. Nothing else leaves.

Evidence: clearAuctionResponse in internal/extension/rfq.go is the whole public surface; amounts and nonces are zeroed the moment a clearing succeeds.

04 / how it works

Four steps, and only one of them trusts the enclave

  1. PostThe maker escrows the lot. The floor is sealed to the enclave key the chain publishes.
  2. SealEach bidder writes a 32-byte commitment on chain, plus a ciphertext only the enclave can open.
  3. ClearAfter the deadline block the enclave decrypts, ranks, and signs the outcome.
  4. SettleThe contract checks the signature and the set, then moves payment and lot together.

Evidence: postRfq, commitBid, requestClearing, relayClearing in contracts/ButaInstructionSender.sol.

05 / the hard part

The bid set cannot be trimmed

The enclave signs over a digest of the commitments the contract recorded, not the ones it was handed. Without that check, whoever calls the clearing hands the enclave a subset, the top bidder clears at its own ask, and the signature is still perfectly valid.

Evidence: if (setDigest != commitmentDigest(rfqId)) revert SetDigestMismatch(); in relayClearing; matchesRecorded() in pkg/auction. Both digests are pinned to the same cross-language test vector.

06 / the second hard part

Screening who can pay, without disclosure

A bidder with no funds wins, the settlement transfer reverts, and the auction dies for the runner-up who could have paid. The enclave already holds the amounts, so it reads balance and allowance for the price owed and passes that bidder over. It tells nobody, including the maker.

Evidence: internal/extension/solvency.go reads balanceOf and allowance for the price owed, never the bid. The bid count reported stays the recorded count, not the survivor count.

08 / what is not done

Said plainly, because you would find it anyway

The enclave runs Flare's simulated TEE path, so the decryption key sits in the process rather than in hardware. The machine is a container behind a reserved tunnel domain, not hosted infrastructure. Testnet tokens. Not audited.

Two limits we found by attacking our own code, both named in the submission: the reserve is the one input that is neither committed nor digested, and a bid that the contract accepts but the enclave cannot open makes the two sets disagree.

09 / next

The shortest path to something a desk could use

Swap the simulated decryptor for the tee-node decrypt path, already wired behind a Decryptor interface, on hosted hardware with a stable hostname. Commit the reserve on chain so it is digested alongside the bids. Then an XRPL delivery leg, and proof of funds through the FDC instead of full escrow.

10 / the sentence to remember

Every sealed-bid venue leaves one reader standing.

Buta makes that reader an attested enclave that is not a party to the trade, cannot be handed a doctored bid set, and keeps nothing.
